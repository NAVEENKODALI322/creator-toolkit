The Tree Concept: A Simple Framework for Building a Real Online Income

By Naveen Kodali, Telugu creator and graphic designer

Jul 27, 2026 · 6 min read

If you have ever searched "how to make money online," you already know the problem: everyone tells you a different platform, a different app, a different trick. What almost nobody explains is the order in which things need to happen. That's what the Tree Concept is for.

Think of a tree. It doesn't start with fruit. It starts underground, where nobody can see it.

Roots: Skills

Every tree needs roots before it can stand upright, and every online income stream needs a skill before it can earn anything. This is the part beginners skip most often, because it's invisible. Nobody claps for you while you're learning video editing, writing better hooks, or understanding basic design principles in Canva.

But roots decide how tall the tree can eventually grow. A creator with weak editing skills and no design sense can still post content — but the ceiling on that content is low. Time spent here isn't wasted time; it's the only investment that compounds every single upload after it.

Practical roots to build first:

  • Basic video editing (cuts, pacing, captions)
  • Simple graphic design (thumbnails, covers, social posts)
  • Writing skills (titles, hooks, descriptions)

Trunk: Consistency

A tree with strong roots but no trunk still falls over. The trunk is what connects what you've learned to the outside world, and in creator terms, the trunk is consistency — showing up on a schedule even when a video underperforms.

Consistency is boring advice, but it's boring because it's true and most people don't do it. Algorithms on every platform reward a track record, not a single lucky post. One video a month for six months will almost always underperform four videos a month for six weeks, even if the total content is similar.

If you want a concrete target: pick a posting frequency you can sustain for 90 days without breaking it once. Not your dream frequency — your realistic one.

Branches: Platform

Branches are where the tree starts reaching outward — this is your platform choice. YouTube, Instagram, or both. This is the step most beginners try to do first, which is like trying to grow branches on a tree with no trunk. It doesn't hold.

Platform choice matters, but it matters less than people think in the beginning. What matters more is picking one primary platform and going deep before spreading across five.

Fruits: Results

Fruits are the views, the subscribers, the brand deals, the ad revenue — the part everyone wants to skip straight to. But fruit only grows on a tree that already has roots, a trunk, and branches. Chasing fruit before building the rest is why so many people quit in month two: they expected results without putting in the earlier stages.

Why This Order Matters

The Tree Concept isn't a motivational metaphor — it's a diagnostic tool. If your channel isn't growing, the answer is almost never "post more." It's usually: which stage am I actually missing?

  • No results, but posting daily? Check your roots (skills) and branches (platform-fit).
  • Good ideas, but nothing published? Check your trunk (consistency).
  • Consistent, but no growth after months? Revisit your roots — the skill gap is likely the ceiling.

Putting It to Work

If you're just getting started, use this framework as a checklist before your next upload. Sharpen one root skill this week — better titles, cleaner thumbnails, or a tighter hook. Growth isn't random. It's a tree — and trees grow from the ground up.
