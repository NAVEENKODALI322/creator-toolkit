Ask ten creators what makes a video get discovered, and most will say thumbnail, title and watch time. Almost nobody says the description. So I did something simple: I opened the descriptions of four of my own most-viewed Telugu videos and checked them against the advice I would give someone else. They did not pass. This post shares what I found and the structure I would use instead.

## What the description does

YouTube reads your description as context. It is one of the clearest signals you can give about what the video is about, beyond the title. An empty or vague description gives YouTube less to work with when it decides who to show your video to.

Descriptions also help viewers directly. Many people read the first lines before they decide to click, and under a video only the first lines show until someone taps "more".

## The first two lines are the most valuable space

Roughly the first 100 to 150 characters show without a click. Those lines have to do two jobs at once: tell a human reader what the video is, and tell YouTube the topic in plain words. A channel intro or a link wastes that space. Put links lower down.

A strong opening answers one question: what is this video, and why should someone watch it?

What I see when I look at it honestly:

1. **None of the four has timestamps.** The videos run 8 to 17 minutes, so a viewer who wants one step has to scrub through the video.
2. **Three of four waste the first line.** Two open with a link and one opens with the word "Description:". Only the delivery video opens by saying what the video is.
3. **The same Telegram promotion block is pasted into two descriptions.** It says nothing about the video it sits under.
4. **The hashtags are weak.** "#money" and "#trending" appear on three of the four and describe nothing. The Fiverr account video has "#teluguchristiansongs", which has nothing to do with Fiverr.

One caution: the Fiverr account video reached about 47K views with that description. A description is one signal among many, and the topic and title matter a lot. I cannot say how many more views a better description would have given it. I can say that my own examples break the rules I would tell a beginner to follow.

## How I would open the Fiverr account description

What it opens with today: "click here to join fiverr and earn", then a link.

What I would write today:

> How to create a Fiverr seller account from India, in Telugu: sign up, set up your profile, publish your first gig and answer the tax questions. Step by step, about 17 minutes.

That one sentence says the topic, the language, the audience and what the video contains. The sign-up link and the Telegram block can follow underneath. I turned the same video into a written guide here: [How I Created My Fiverr Seller Account and First Gig](/blog/how-to-create-fiverr-account-and-first-gig.html).

## A structure that works

1. **Opening hook (1 to 2 lines):** what the video covers and why it matters, written for a person.
2. **Short summary (2 to 4 lines):** a little more detail, with the keywords appearing naturally.
3. **Timestamps:** these also create chapters in the video's progress bar. YouTube's rules are that the first timestamp must be 00:00, you need at least three, and each chapter must be at least 10 seconds long.
4. **Links and calls to action:** your social links, related videos, a subscribe prompt.
5. **Hashtags:** three to five that really describe the video, at the end. I wrote more on this in [Hashtags Won't Save a Bad Title](/blog/youtube-hashtag-strategy-2026.html).

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Leaving it blank or one line.** It costs nothing to fix.
- **Pasting the same block into every video.** A shared footer for your social links is fine. A shared body gives no context about this video.
- **Keyword stuffing.** Repeating a phrase five times reads as spam to people.
- **Burying the point.** If a viewer has to read to the end to learn what the video is about, the description is not doing its job.
- **Hashtags that do not match the video.** A wrong hashtag sends the wrong signal.

## A small habit

A proper description takes two or three extra minutes per upload. Over fifty videos that is a real difference in how clearly your channel explains itself.
