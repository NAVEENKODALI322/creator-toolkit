My Canva Creator Program Earnings: Real Numbers From 2025 and 2026

By Naveen Kodali, Telugu creator and graphic designer

Oct 5, 2026 · 4 min read

Most posts about the Canva Creator Program talk in theory: "upload consistently and you will earn passive income." I wanted to write the version I never found when I started: real numbers from my own dashboard, including the months that were disappointing.

I'm Naveen Kodali, a graphic designer from Andhra Pradesh. I make Telugu and Indian-themed elements for Canva, and I make YouTube videos about Canva for Telugu creators. Here is exactly what the program has paid me so far.

The numbers, straight from my dashboard

2025 (full year):

  • Total earnings: US$508.68
  • Royalty earnings: US$467.18
  • Monthly range: roughly US$23 to US$53
  • Average: about US$39 per month

2026 (January to August shown so far):

  • Total earnings: US$243.45
  • Royalty earnings: US$234.93
  • Monthly range: roughly US$19 to US$61
  • Average: about US$29 per month

If you only looked at one month, you could think this program pays $20, or you could think it pays $60. Both are true for me. That swing is the most important thing I can tell you about it.

Why my earnings go up and down

Canva's dashboard says it plainly: earnings are based on how often your templates and elements are used. It also says that only Pro elements accrue royalties. So your income is not tied to how many things you upload on a given day. It is tied to how many Canva users actually use your work, and that changes every month.

In my own numbers, 2025 was fairly steady, with a peak around the middle of the year and a drop toward the end. In 2026, the first five months stayed low, and then June and July jumped. July 2026 was my best month so far at about US$61, and August fell back to roughly US$24.

I cannot control which month spikes. I have no formula for predicting it. If someone tells you they can predict their Canva royalties month to month, be careful.

What I actually upload

I have 246 graphics on my Canva creator profile. A lot of them are things generic stock libraries do not cover well:

  • Telugu lettering and typography elements, such as festival titles
  • Indian illustrations, like traditional-style character art
  • Festival and national-day graphics, including Independence Day designs

My background is in print and packaging design and font design, so I naturally think about lettering, clean shapes, and colors that work on a real layout. That is the part of my work that is hard to copy, and it is where I put most of my effort.

What I think matters (and what I am not sure about)

1. A specific niche beats generic content. Canva already has thousands of generic flowers, frames, and gradients. Fewer creators make good Telugu lettering. I cannot prove this is why my work earns, but it is the direction I keep pushing.

2. Volume matters, but only slowly. Each element earns very little by itself. A library of a few hundred elements lets small amounts add up. I would not expect a single upload to change anything.

3. Quality control is not optional. Canva reviews every submission, and clean, consistent files get through more easily. Reading rejection reasons carefully saves a lot of time.

4. Seasonal designs can help, but they are not magic. I make festival and national-day graphics because people search for them. Even so, my best month in 2026 was not followed by another strong month. Do not build your plan around one spike.

How I think about this income

I treat Canva royalties as a bonus, not a salary. Some months it is about US$20. Some months it is US$50 or US$60. That unpredictability is real, but the money still helps, and it keeps coming in from work I uploaded months ago.

If you are expecting thousands of dollars a month in your first year, my numbers will disappoint you. If you want a small, slow-growing income from design work you already enjoy doing, they might look more reasonable.

The unexpected bonus: YouTube

I also make YouTube videos about Canva for Telugu creators. These videos consistently get better CTR and more views than my other topics. I did not plan this. The Canva audience in Telugu is hungry for practical, honest content, and my real experience gives me something to talk about.

So the program has two benefits for me: the royalty income, and a topic that my YouTube audience actually watches.

If you are starting out

  • Pick one niche you can make better than average, and stay with it for a few months.
  • Upload steadily, but do not rush quality for volume.
  • Do not judge the program by one month. Look at six months together.
  • Keep your files clean and read every rejection reason.
  • Expect small, uneven payouts, and be happy when they grow.

I will keep updating this post with new numbers as the months go by. If you have questions about the Canva Creator Program, leave a comment on my YouTube channel @naveenkodali322 and I will answer in a video.
