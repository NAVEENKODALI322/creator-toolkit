Why translate your title and description?

YouTube lets you add your video's title and description in other languages. Viewers whose YouTube is set to that language see the translated version, and the translated words can help your video show up when they search in their own language. The video stays the same. India has viewers in many languages, so a cooking video in Hindi, a tech review in Telugu or a Canva tutorial in Tamil can find extra viewers who understand the topic but search in a different language.

How to add the translations in YouTube Studio

  1. Open YouTube Studio and choose Subtitles from the left menu.
  2. Click the video, then Add language and choose the language.
  3. Under Title & description, click Add.
  4. Paste the translated title and description from this tool and click Publish.

Repeat for each language. The menu names can change slightly as YouTube updates Studio, but translations are always managed per language under Subtitles.

Which languages should you pick?

  • Neighbouring audiences: a Telugu video often reaches Tamil and Kannada viewers who watch for the visuals. A Hindi video can reach Bhojpuri, Marathi and Punjabi viewers.
  • English: useful when the topic is a tool or app that people search for in English anywhere in India.
  • Your analytics: in YouTube Studio, check the Audience tab for the viewer languages and regions that already watch you, and translate for those first.

You can pick up to six languages at a time. The tool remembers your choice for next time.

What the translator keeps and changes

Translations are written the way native speakers search, not word for word. Brand and app names like YouTube, Canva or Instagram stay in English. Links, @handles, #hashtags, emojis and timestamps such as 0:00 stay exactly as they are, so your chapters keep working. Titles are kept under YouTube's 100-character limit with the main keyword near the start.

Check before you publish

AI translation is good, not perfect, especially for slang and less common languages. If you know someone who speaks the language, ask them to read the title. If a translation looks off, translate again or keep the English title for that language. Use the Title & Description Checker to see how a translated title looks on a phone.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does adding translations change my original video?

No. The video and your original title stay the same. Viewers whose YouTube language matches a translation see the translated title and description.

Which languages can I translate into?

English and 13 Indian languages: Hindi, Bengali, Marathi, Telugu, Tamil, Gujarati, Urdu, Kannada, Odia, Malayalam, Punjabi, Assamese and Bhojpuri. Pick up to six at a time.

Will my hashtags, links and timestamps change?

No. They are kept exactly as they are, so links work and chapters still line up.

Where do I paste the translations?

In YouTube Studio, open Subtitles, choose the video, add the language, then add the title and description for it.

Is it free?

Yes, it is free and needs no sign-up.
